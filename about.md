<h1 class="post-title about-title">About</h1>

I'm Daniel Whicker, based in Austin, Texas. I'm a Principal Consultant, and these
days most of my work is automating infrastructure: VM migrations, new hardware
deployments, CI/CD pipelines, and the Kubernetes underneath them.

I've been in infrastructure for close to thirty years. I started on IBM's AIX
support line in 1996, spent a long stretch on storage, backup and clustering, and
ended up in automation. I've mentored a lot of people along the way. My
[resume](https://bifrost.cc/resume.html) has the details.

Before any of that, the first computer in the house was the family Commodore 128.
Later I got my own, a used Commodore 64, and used it to call BBSes. Then I ran one
for a while, The Abyss. I didn't get a PC until I started at UT in 1992, a Packard
Bell 386sx20, and that's where I discovered Linux. In 1992! Slackware 0.99-something,
I believe.

I'm a terminal nerd. Given a GUI way or a TUI way to do something, I'll usually
pick the TUI. I started with `screen` on vt100 terminals hooked up to old Sun
SPARCstations (the 1, IPC, IPX), and I never grew up much past that. I even worked
for Sun, before Oracle borked them. I live in iTerm2. I've tried Ghostty, Wave and
the rest, and their biggest fault is that they're not iTerm2.

At home I run a lot more than a house needs: Uptime Kuma, Home Assistant, HomeKit
(we're an Apple household), and an arcade cabinet that got its own automation. Most
of what's on this site started there.

## Outside work

- **Cruising through life with my wife.** Here's the
  [cruise history](https://bifrost.cc/history.html).
- **Old games.** I still play Colonization IV regularly, plus Command & Conquer
  through OpenRA, RollerCoaster Tycoon through OpenRCT2, and Ultima VII through
  Exult. Tetris on a Game Boy (the original DMG-01) and RC Pro-Am on the NES too,
  and plenty more.
- **Building retro gaming stuff.** Several retro gaming computers, an arcade
  cabinet, handhelds, and so on.
- **Photography.** I used to do it as a hobby. I wasn't very good, and modern
  iPhones just make it all happen for you.

## About this site

I write for people who already know their way around a terminal, and I try to keep
it short. I write about what a project does and why I wanted it, and I link the
code when it's public.

Everything here is my own work and my own opinion -- and it's probably just wrong.
